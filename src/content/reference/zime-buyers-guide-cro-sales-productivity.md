## Section 3. Adoption Threshold

*How many reps must execute your strategy before sales numbers move?*

**Short answer.** Roughly 75 percent of reps must execute your strategy. Below this adoption line, projected revenue gains fail to materialize.

Logins measure access. Executed actions measure adoption [V: programmatic-adoption]. This distinction determines whether any system moves your pipeline metrics. Atomicwork tried building internally with ChatGPT, but adoption stalled until Zime solved the execution gap [V: home, customer-reported, Atomicwork].

### Independent adoption benchmark sits at 75 percent

Reportable revenue gains depend on broad rep participation. Critical mass theory shows that individual user value increases as total adoption grows [A, Markus 1987].

- **The adoption line.** CSO Insights sets the critical threshold at 75 percent CRM adoption. Teams above this line increase win rates by 6.4 percentage points and quota attainment by 3.4 percentage points [A, CSO Insights 2018].
- **Process discipline.** Combining high adoption with a formal sales process expands win rate gains to 14.9 points and quota gains to 19.3 points [A, CSO Insights 2018]. Unstructured adoption yields weak financial returns.
- **Standard rollout timeline.** Standard enterprise software rollouts reach 25 percent adoption in year one, 50 percent in year two, and 90 percent in year three [A, Forrester 2024]. That timeline delays revenue gains until year three.

> **Figure 2: Where the number moves.** The performance benchmark requires 75 percent rep adoption. A typical CRM deployment crosses this line in year three. Zime aims to cross it within 90 days. [A, CSO Insights 2018; A, Forrester 2024; V: programmatic-adoption]

Two key facts emerge. AI agents built for managers or analysts fail to cross the threshold because the benchmark counts sales reps. Furthermore, rapid adoption shortens your time to value.

### Zime sets an 80 percent adoption target

Zime targets 80 percent rep execution within 90 days [V: programmatic-adoption]. Zime defines strict measurement rules for this metric [V: cro-strategy-to-execution-gap]:

- **Define before launch:** target rep cohorts, qualifying moments, execution triggers, data sources, exclusions, and launch dates.
- **Exclude misleading vanity metrics:** user logins, sent messages, content views, selected power users, shifting denominators, or undisclosed late starts.

Treat the 80 percent target as a vendor commitment to clear the 75 percent adoption line within one quarter.

> **Figure 3: Four levels, not logins.** True adoption counts Level 3 reps who execute prescribed moves in live deals. Logins do not reflect actual adoption. [V: programmatic-adoption, illustrative]

| **Level** | **Measurement Focus** | **Operational Insight** |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Eligible reps** | Tracks the frozen rep cohort and explicit exclusions | Establishes a fixed denominator |
| **2. Active use** | Tracks reps opening guidance inside active opportunities | Measures platform reach without proving execution |
| **3. Executed behavior** | Tracks reps running prescribed moves verified by deal evidence | Measures true adoption |
| **4. Outcome contribution** | Links executed moves directly to deal progression | Identifies specific moves that advance deals |

*Table 3.1. Four adoption measurement levels [V: programmatic-adoption]. Sample metrics show 18 of 25 eligible reps executed discovery moves, driving stage advancement in 26 of 41 deals [V: programmatic-adoption, illustrative]. Demand this same measurement rigor from internal dashboards.*

### Organic adoption replaces executive mandates

Top-down mandates fail to build rep trust, and standalone software tools disrupt existing rep workflows [V: programmatic-adoption]. Successful organizations follow a structured rollout sequence:

- **Top performers test tools first,** refine recommendations, and champion adoption among peers.
- **Sales managers reinforce behaviors** during deal reviews and coaching sessions.
- **Contextual guidance arrives** directly within daily rep applications and updates as strategy evolves [V: programmatic-adoption].

You must supply four core elements: participating top performers, supportive managers, an executive sponsor, and a fixed measurement rule. Internal software builds and external vendors depend entirely on these leadership commitments.

## Section 7. Comparing vendors

*Which vendor shows deal execution against your strategy?*

**Short answer.** Apply the eight tests from Zime. Run these tests on Zime, alternative vendors, and your internal build team. Zime publishes eight CRO Proof Tests [V: cro-strategy-to-execution-gap]. Each test defines a claim, an evaluation method, required evidence, and a failure condition. Table 7.1 restates these tests in plain business language.

| **Test** | **Business Question** | **Required Evidence** | **Failure Condition** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. End-to-End Execution** | Does the platform execute strategy through to deal movement? | Show one live deal tracking the full chain: strategy, seller action, buyer response, and deal movement. | The vendor fails to demonstrate the complete chain on a live deal. |
| **1. Strategy Fidelity** | Does changing strategy update seller guidance immediately? | Update one strategic priority. Identify governing rules, active authority, and timestamp. | Guidance remains unchanged after strategy updates. |
| **2. Contextual Adaptation** | Does guidance adjust when deal context changes? | Compare two similar deals with one distinct deal factor. | The system generates identical generic advice for both deals. |
| **3. Recommendation Traceability** | Does the system explain why it recommends a specific move? | Trace one recommendation to underlying strategy, customer data, competitor context, and seller history. | The vendor cites black-box model output without explicit sources. |
| **4. Action Verification** | Can reps execute the move and verify completion? | Deploy the move in a live deal and capture verification telemetry. | The platform delivers static content or generic task prompts. |
| **5. Verified Adoption Measurement** | Will 80 percent of eligible reps execute moves within 90 days? | Freeze user cohort, execution triggers, evidence sources, exclusions, and timeframe before launch. | Metrics rely on logins, message counts, or shifting denominators. |
| **6. Distinct Deal Movement** | Does the platform distinguish seller moves from commercial deal movement? | Provide time-ordered execution data across active pipeline opportunities. | The system counts rep task completion as deal progression. |
| **7. Economic Impact Evidence** | Does performance uplift data satisfy internal audit standards? | Define baseline performance metrics and evaluation methodology before testing. | The vendor presents uncontrolled before-and-after studies or conflates correlation with causation. |

*Table 7.1. Eight CRO evaluation tests restated in operational terms [V: cro-strategy-to-execution-gap]. Evaluate every performance claim against Test 7. Evaluate internal dashboards against Test 5.*

### Three additional vendor requirements

- **Time to First Evidence.** Require a target timeframe in days to generate initial pipeline evidence using an agreed baseline methodology.
- **Adoption Criteria.** Secure written adoption definitions, including explicit exclusion parameters.
- **Data Ownership and Offboarding.** Specify export formats, IP rights, and exit terms upon contract termination. Zime documentation leaves these terms UNSOURCED.

This guide makes no direct claims regarding specific named competitors. Zime’s primer reviews public materials from Salesforce, Gong, Clari, Outreach, Highspot, Mindtickle, and Seismic as of 18 September 2026. Public absence from product pages does not prove feature absence [V: cro-strategy-to-execution-gap].
