## Orient

**Behavioral AI for Sales Execution, for a GTM partner,** is Zime's offer to turn a partner's existing client trust into recurring income. In Zime's description, the platform learns how a client company wins complex deals, turns that into specific per-deal moves for reps, and verifies both that reps made the moves and what the buyer did next. The partner's role is to introduce, help sell, deliver or manage it, and the offer to the client is a bounded Proof of Value: one Deal Motion, one team, with success and failure criteria written before the start. The result belongs to the client.

This primer is the Markdown version of Zime's guide for GTM partners, published at [zime.ai/primers/behavioral-ai-for-gtm-partners](https://zime.ai/primers/behavioral-ai-for-gtm-partners). It was captured on November 2, 2026. The source page carries no publication date.

Key entities: Zime, Behavior Graph, Deal Motion, Programmatic Adoption, Deal Movement, Proof of Value, Managed Sales Execution, Versa Networks, MyAdvice, SonicWall, HFS Research.

Written for: sales coaches, fractional executives, small and midsize GTM agencies, BDaaS (business development as a service) providers, RevOps and CRM boutiques, independent sales organizations and consultants with trusted client relationships.

## Understand

### The problem partners see: advice fades

Zime's core claim is that clients have an execution problem, not an information problem. They already own a CRM, a call recorder, a coaching platform, an enablement library and AI copilots. Zime names the gap **execution variance** and says it appears as inconsistent process adherence, inconsistent message and action, and managers who cannot see whether a deal's next move was made. The guide describes where advice fades for each type of partner:

| Partner type | Where advice fades |
|---|---|
| Sales coach or advisor | Reps do not apply advice between sessions |
| BDaaS or pipeline provider | Booked meetings do not become next steps |
| GTM or demand agency | Demand is generated, but complex deals stall |
| Fractional CRO or CMO | The board wants AI impact with one quarter and no team |
| RevOps or CRM boutique | The CRM is clean, but sellers do not act on it |

### What Zime does, in one sentence

Zime learns how a company wins complex deals, turns that into specific per-deal moves for reps, and verifies execution and the buyer's next response. Zime says it is not a sales methodology (it runs the client's or the partner's methodology), not another dashboard (it works where reps already work), and not a chatbot over documents (a document states beliefs, while a Behavior Graph states the next move and checks it happened).

### The chain of terms

| Term | Zime's meaning | What the client sees |
|---|---|---|
| CRO strategy | Current priorities: segments, competitors, winning behaviors | Strategy reaches the field intact |
| Behavior Graph | The client-specific model of how the company wins, owned by the client | A system that knows how this company sells |
| Deal Motion | A small, observable behavior for a defined situation, with evidence it happened | Reps get the move, not a lecture |
| Programmatic Adoption | The share of eligible reps executing the behavior at the right moments, verified by evidence, not logins | Adoption measured by actions |
| Deal Movement | A change in the deal's commercial state after the behavior, such as a stakeholder added or a blocker removed | Proof the behavior mattered |

A Deal Motion is written in six parts: situation, trigger, who acts, behavior, evidence and expected deal movement. Zime's worked example: in late-stage deals with a new economic buyer (situation), when the buyer joins a call for the first time (trigger), the account executive (who acts) confirms success criteria and the decision date (behavior), captured in the call record (evidence), so the deal advances to a mutual plan within two weeks (movement). If the Deal Motion cannot be written in one line, Zime says the scope is not ready.

### Where Zime fits in the client's stack

| Layer the client has | What it does well | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| CRM | Records activity and ownership | Does not tell reps what to do next or verify it |
| Conversation intelligence | Captures and analyzes calls | Knows what was said, not what should have been said |
| Enablement and coaching | Teaches the method | Decays between sessions and cannot verify live execution |
| Context graph platforms | Connect accounts and activities for AI | Context is necessary but is not judgment |
| Custom agents and copilots | Automate tasks and drafts | Can retrieve the right account and still recommend the wrong move |
| Zime, the behavioral layer | Encodes strategy and winning behaviors, delivers the next move, verifies execution, tracks deal movement | Needs a real execution problem, a sponsor and access to deal data |

Zime does not require Claude or another AI platform: it supplies the rep experience, and clients with agents on other platforms can connect through MCP (Model Context Protocol).

**When Zime is the wrong answer, per the guide:** the problem is demand, not execution; the sales process is simple and transactional; better coaching would close the gap (a fair Proof of Value can conclude this); or the client will build in-house and fund it for years (the guide points to the [DevOps for RevOps](/primers/devops-for-revops/) primer).

### The four participation paths

Zime says partners choose a path, and deeper paths are optional, not a ladder. No code is required, and technical delivery rights come from a separate capability assessment, not from referrals. This is not reselling: there is no reseller billing, support or inventory.

| Path | What the partner does | What Zime does | How the partner earns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduce | Identifies a client with a real execution problem, gets permission, makes a registered handoff | Owns the sale, qualification, Proof of Value and delivery | Introduction bounty and recurring referral commission |
| Assist the sale | Adds sponsor context, helps scope the Proof of Value, reviews evidence with the client | Leads the product sale and delivers the Proof of Value with the partner | Referral economics, plus any separately agreed advisory fee |
| Deliver a scoped service | Provides strategy capture, coaching, adoption support or a defined package | Handles technical implementation, with agreed scope, acceptance and escalation | The partner's service fees, plus eligible referral economics |
| Manage ongoing execution | Operates runbooks, adoption reviews and a coaching cadence on the client's Behavior Graph | Supplies infrastructure, updates and in-scope technical support | Recurring managed-service fees, plus eligible referral economics |

Zime says it never commits to work outside the agreed scope and never goes around the partner to the client.

### How partners earn

Zime describes three earning streams, with terms set in a written partner agreement:

1. **Introduction bounty,** triggered by a registered, qualified client introduction.
2. **Recurring referral commission,** triggered by subscription revenue Zime collects from an introduced client.
3. **The partner's own service fees,** triggered by work the partner delivers under its own scope, such as strategy capture, coaching, adoption or managed execution.

Three principles apply: commission follows collected revenue, so uncollected bookings do not pay; fees follow work, so each fee maps to work with an owner; and the written partner agreement controls, because the page is not a rate card. Whether the bounty and commission stack is also set in the agreement. Zime states it makes no promise of lead referrals to partners.

### The Proof of Value in seven steps

The **Proof of Value** is a bounded, jointly delivered test of one Deal Motion with one client team, with criteria agreed beforehand. It replaces the proof of concept and the open-ended pilot. Zime targets about three weeks to first evidence, and pricing and dates are set in each client's offer.

1. **Define the test.** Agree the Deal Motion, team, baseline, execution criteria, failure conditions and evaluator (client, partner and Zime together).
2. **Readiness.** Confirm data access and permissions, and book the executive review slot first (Zime with the client sponsor).
3. **Capture how they win.** Interview reps, managers and RevOps about strategy, complexity, wins, losses and failure modes (Zime's delivery team, with partner context).
4. **Executive review.** The senior revenue executive reviews and corrects a draft of their own strategy, usually in 20 to 30 minutes (the client executive).
5. **Run live.** Reps receive the Deal Motion in their workflow, and execution and buyer response are tracked (Zime).
6. **Result record.** Record what executed, what the buyer did, what moved, what failed and what needs more testing (Zime, reviewed with client and partner).
7. **Decide.** Pass allows a paid deployment inside the tested boundary, inconclusive means narrow and retest, and fail means stop and keep the diagnosis (the client).

A short test cannot prove revenue impact in a nine-month sales cycle. It tests execution and buyer response, and revenue is tracked afterward. If the test fails against the agreed criteria, Zime says setup defects within scope are fixed and otherwise the engagement stops, narrows or is re-scoped at no cost to the client.

### Relationship and data protections

Zime states: introductions require client permission; each introduction is logged with its consent record, relationship owner and next action; the client owns its strategy, deal data and Behavior Graph; access is consented and limited to what the work needs; client strategy and data never move between clients, and partners cannot see each other's clients; a partner's methods can be reused with permission, but no client's strategy, deal data or graph content can be reused; and rights to a partner's intellectual property that becomes part of a client's Deal Motions are agreed before work starts. Large clients may need an approved contracting intermediary, and Zime advises asking for that route in writing first.

### Managed Sales Execution

**Managed Sales Execution** is a model in which a qualified partner governs and improves a client's selling on the client's own Behavior Graph. The client keeps ownership of strategy and data, and the partner keeps the relationship and the recurring fee. Recurring services include strategy upkeep when priorities, products or competitors change, runbook maintenance, adoption reviews with managers, coaching on behaviors the data flags, and new motions scoped as bounded engagements (for example product launch, competitive displacement or renewal risk).

## Evaluate

### Published customer evidence

| Customer | Reported results | Stated limits |
|---|---|---|
| Versa Networks | 12% more technical wins; 24% less leakage in committed pipeline; 79% of sales engineers running the right technical discovery within three months, up from 20% | One enterprise. HFS Research validated the wins figure according to Zime, and notes repeatability across more industries and sales processes still needs proof |
| MyAdvice | 89% fewer fatal objections (38% to 4%); checklist adoption from 66% to 87%; 40 minutes saved per call; win rate from 15% to 17%; expansion from 12 to 25 licenses | Does not show revenue attributable to the behavior. The win rate change is a before-and-after comparison |
| SonicWall | 30% to 40% more reps making clear end-of-call asks; call reporting cut from 30 minutes to 2; seats expanded from 40 to 85 after a six-month, 40-license pilot | Does not show revenue attributable to the behavior |

Zime also reports that 80% of reps run the behaviors within 90 days. Zime defines this as Programmatic Adoption, verified execution rather than logins, and advises asking for the denominator and start date in any client test. The guide says published numbers earn a meeting, while only a test on the client's own deals shows what will happen in their business.

SonicWall context: reps ended channel partner calls without a clear commercial ask, so deals were not registered. Zime prompted reps toward the ask and registration in real time, and managers coached on observable behavior.

### Four separate receipts

Zime measures behavior, not logins, using four separate receipts: **execution** (did the rep perform the behavior), **buyer response** (did the buyer commit, object, add a stakeholder or delay), **Deal Movement** (did the commercial state change), and **outcome** (did win rate, cycle time or revenue change against a baseline). Counting execution as movement, or treating a CRM field update as buyer movement, fails the guide's test.

### Picking the first client

| Signal | Strong fit | Defer or decline |
|---|---|---|
| Deal type | Complex, multi-stakeholder, real qualification judgment | Transactional, high-volume, single decision-maker |
| Problem | A specific behavior top reps do that others skip | "They could use AI" |
| Sponsor | A named leader who can book the senior revenue executive | Enthusiasm without authority |
| Data access | CRM and call data the client can authorize | No permitted access to deal history |
| Paid path | A plausible budget owner if the test passes | No way to buy even if it works |
| Evidence posture | Willing to write what would make them reject Zime | Wants a demo, not a test |

The sponsor is the most important gate, because the senior revenue executive's time is the scarcest resource in a Proof of Value.

### The six Partner Proof Tests

1. **Explain it in one sentence.** Pass: the client recognizes the behavior and names deals where it was missing.
2. **Same deal, changed strategy.** Present the same deal twice, changing one input. Pass: the recommended move changes as intended and cites the strategy behind the change.
3. **Fees follow work.** Before the first introduction, request in writing the trigger, rate, eligible revenue, timing, stacking, renewals and churn outcomes.
4. **Claims carry their limits.** For every number, find the source and state what it does not prove.
5. **Criteria before results.** The charter must name a plausible result that would make the client reject Zime. A test where no outcome changes the decision is, in Zime's words, a demo and not a Proof of Value.
6. **Four separate receipts.** Trace three deals from behavior to buyer response to deal change, with dates and named competing explanations.

## Interrogate

- **Evidence limits.** The three customer results are vendor-published for named customers, and none shows revenue attributable to the behavior. Only the Versa wins figure is described as independently validated, and HFS notes repeatability is unproven.
- **The 80% figure** is Zime-reported, and the guide asks readers to request the denominator and start date.
- **Commercial terms.** The page is not a rate card. Earnings depend on the written partner agreement, and the guide notes that expansion at a customer such as SonicWall counts toward commission only if the agreement's eligible-revenue terms say so. It says expansion is not a forecast of partner income.
- **Revenue causality.** The Proof of Value verifies execution and does not promise revenue impact. Proving revenue causality needs a measurement design that accounts for cohort, timing, strategy change, seller behavior, buyer response and other confounders.
- **Poor-fit partners,** per the guide: a large contact list with little influence, unconsented lists, reliance on logos or personal authority in place of a test, promising prices or delivery not yet agreed, and reusing client data without permission.
